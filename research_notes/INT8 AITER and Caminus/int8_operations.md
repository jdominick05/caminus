# INT8 AITER math and fit for Caminus

## What the fork actually computes

### Takeaway

The fork began as a W8A8 MI100 serving port, but current source has advanced beyond its README: packed GGUF IQ2_XXS/Q8_0 and NVFP4 decoding, grouped INT8 computation, bounded expanded-weight caches, fused output quantization and native INT8 attention. This makes it more relevant to Caminus than a generic INT8 library, while still requiring a hardware port and new numerical validation. [README](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/README.md); [Packed operators](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py); [Native operators](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/int8_native.py)

### Cited findings

- Snapshot: fork `main` resolved to `cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3`, committed 2026-11-12 16:29:50 UTC. All fork source links use this immutable commit. No builds, installations or GPU runs were performed in this investigation. [Commit](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/commit/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3)

- The README's original target is Qwen3.8-27B-class W8A8 GPTQ weights, dynamic per-token activation quantization, TP4 on four MI100s, DFlash2 and graph-captured vLLM execution. It describes the CK path's scale contract as FP32 activation `[M,1]` and weight-row `[N,1]` scales. That is **W8A8**, not W8A16: floating activations entering a layer are quantized before the integer matrix multiply. [README serving and CK sections](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/README.md)

- Its CK correctness script tests M in `{1,8,64,512,4096}` and matrix pairs `(K,N)` of `(5120,5120)`, `(5120,17408)` and `(17408,5120)`. The reference is **dequantized already-quantized inputs**, `(xq.float()*xs) @ (wq.float()*ws).T`, with a mean relative error threshold of `1e-3`. Therefore the README's approximately `1.8e-4` figure measures GEMM/epilogue agreement with quantized operands, not the full activation/weight quantization loss or model quality. Its reported 1.8–6.8x speedups are author measurements on that stack, not measurements on Caminus or RX7900. [Actual test](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/op_tests/test_gemm_a8w8_int8_gfx908.py#L19-L31)

- New packed interfaces explicitly accept three storage codecs: `iq2_xxs`, `q8_0`, and `nvfp4`. IQ2_XXS is `[N,K/256,66]` bytes, Q8_0 `[N,K/32,34]`, and NVFP4 packed `[N,K/2]` with separate E4M3 subblock scales and FP32 global scale(s). These are actual codec implementations; the IQ2 path decodes codebook indices, sign parity and the FP16 block scale. [Interface and geometry](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py#L89-L148); [IQ2/Q8 decoders](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/_triton_kernels/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py#L9-L43)

- **Packed storage is retained, but the multiply changes its math.** Each 128-element activation group is dynamically quantized with `sx=max(maxabs(x)/127,1e-30)` and nearest-integer codes. Decoded weights are separately requantized with one `sw` per output row per 128 K elements. It computes `dot=INT32_dot(xq,wq)` and accumulates `float(dot)*sx*sw` in FP32 across groups. Expanded cache hits reuse the same weight codes and group scales instead of decoding again. This is not merely lossless byte rearrangement. [Activation quantization](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/_triton_kernels/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py#L82-L89); [Weight requantization and accumulation](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/_triton_kernels/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py#L159-L192)

- The new standalone HIP small-M kernel implements the same group-scaled integer equation. Its source calls `__builtin_amdgcn_mfma_i32_16x16x16i8`, with wave64 lane-fragment mappings and four INT32 accumulators per lane. This is native CDNA1 matrix INT8 MFMA, not a software FP8-to-FP16 workaround or a scalar `dot4` loop. Packed kernels likewise decode four values into a register fragment, requantize and feed that MFMA instruction. [MFMA primitive](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/int8_mfma_lane.cuh#L4-L15); [Actual builtin](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/int8_mfma_lane.cuh#L29-L61); [Grouped GEMM equation and layouts](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/gemm_a8w8_m16_gfx908.hip#L4-L17); [Packed MFMA call](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/gemm_packed_g128_gfx908.hip#L18-L82)

- Packed dense dispatch uses native HIP at eligible small M (`M<=64`, aligned slices and certain epilogue/codec conditions), otherwise Triton. Routed IQ2 experts use native HIP for small M with 16-row route tiles, and Triton for larger prefills or direct-INT8 variants. The cache stores selected experts as INT8 weights, ranks usage, preserves surviving slots and refreshes according to a configurable period. This amortizes expensive codebook decode at the cost of extra memory and cache-maintenance work. [Dense dispatch](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py#L153-L209); [Routed dispatch](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py#L321-L365); [Expert cache implementation](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/packed_expert_cache.py)

- The newer fused projection path can apply SiLU, optionally multiply by an up projection, and quantize the output to GS128 INT8 in one Triton kernel. There is also a floating-input/weight projection followed by an INT8 output epilogue. Consequently, not every fork operation is an integer GEMM: the floating projection remains floating math and only changes its output representation. The latest commit's tuning targets an M=96 shared-expert shape, not single-token decoding alone. [SiLU/quant output](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/gemm_silu_quant_out.py); [Floating projection/quant epilogue](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/gemm_float_quant_out.py); [Latest tuning commit](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/commit/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3)

- There is also **INT8 KV attention**, separate from W8A8 linear layers. `paged_attn_decode_int8` accepts BF16 Q and per-token-head INT8 K/V with FP32 scales, quantizes Q and uses integer QK/PV MFMA. The source folds V scales into probabilities and requantizes probabilities to INT8 before PV, adding another approximation. It uses FP32 running softmax/statistics/accumulation and produces BF16 output. An optional caller-applied Hadamard transform is supported. Its cache representation differs from Caminus's per-32-value Q8_0 blocks. [Attention wrapper](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/int8_native.py#L195-L263); [Attention kernel math](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/paged_attn_decode_int8_pthead_gfx908.hip#L4-L19); [Probability requantization](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/paged_attn_decode_int8_pthead_gfx908.hip#L282-L333); [Local KV byte calculation](../../python/caminus/engine/k3.py)

### Inferences

- Keeping a GGUF file intact does not preserve its original numerical path if every tile is decoded and requantized to GS128. It may preserve storage compatibility and bounded source memory, but changes represented multiplication operands, activation quantization, rounding and reductions. Q8_0's four independent 32-value scales are also regrouped into a shared 128-weight scale in this path. These changes need model-level evidence, not only equality between cached/uncached versions of the same new math. [Requantization source](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/_triton_kernels/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py#L159-L192); [Cache tests compare new paths](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/op_tests/test_packed_expert_cache_gfx908.py#L132-L175)

### Gaps

- No independently verified end-to-end accuracy or speed results on Caminus's target GGUFs and cards were produced.
- The README refers to `GFX908_BUILD_PLAN.md`, but that path was absent in the inspected current tree. Its claims that work remains only a plan cannot override the implemented grouped/native paths cited above.

## Hardware and source portability

### Takeaway

The new hand-tuned kernel work targets MI100 gfx908. RDNA3 relevance is architectural inspiration and potential source porting, not binary compatibility. The fork's broad inherited AITER catalogue and any inherited gfx1100 support must not be mistaken for verification of these new packed/native paths. [Native HIP loader](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/gemm_packed_native.py#L15-L45); [CK support gate](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/gemm_op_a8w8.py#L38-L60)

### Cited findings

- The packed HIP loader hardcodes `--offload-arch=gfx908`. The small-M packed dispatch checks geometry/epilogue, **not the actual GPU architecture**, before calling it. Therefore an apparent generic Python entrypoint does not guarantee it safely falls back to Triton on RX7900. Its native branch needs an actual architecture gate or a dedicated port. [Hardcoded build target](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/gemm_packed_native.py#L15-L45); [Shape-based dispatch](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py#L173-L209)

- `int8_native.py` has opt-in sites controlled by `AITER_INT8_NATIVE`, but its `_arch()` reads an environment override defaulting to gfx908; it is not itself a runtime gfx1100 detector. Switching an environment string is not an implementation of RDNA3 lane mappings or instructions. [Build and environment architecture](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/int8_native.py#L24-L71); [Opt-in sites](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/int8_native.py#L118-L147)

- The regular CK W8A8 gate states that its prebuilt CK/ASM code objects are gfx9/CDNA-only and returns false on gfx11/gfx12; other architectures should use the Triton path. This is not evidence that all new fork paths share the same guard. [CK guard](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/gemm_op_a8w8.py#L54-L60)

- The native math explicitly assumes wave64 CDNA fragment layout and MI100 MFMA. The code is not an RDNA WMMA or scalar packed-dot implementation. Existing gfx1151/gfx1201 or other configs inherited elsewhere in the tree do not establish portability of this source to gfx1100. [Fragment layout and opcode](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/int8_mfma_lane.cuh#L4-L15); [Wave constant/builtin](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/int8_mfma_lane.cuh#L29-L61)

### Inferences

- The worthwhile RDNA investigation is: implement the same group-scaled INT8 algorithm using an instruction/layout actually supported on gfx1100, then compare it with existing WMMA/dequant and VALU paths. The fork's code cannot establish which route is faster on RDNA3. [Fork MFMA design](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/gemm_a8w8_m16_gfx908.hip); [Local WMMA prefill](../../python/caminus/prefill/q35own.py)
- The work avoids requiring native FP8 arithmetic on MI100. It does not prove that INT8 beats FP16 on Caminus: conversions, source decode, scale application, smaller batch geometry and bandwidth still determine the outcome. [NVFP4 byte decoding comment](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/_triton_kernels/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py#L47-L78); [Native GEMM equation](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/gemm_a8w8_m16_gfx908.hip#L4-L17)

### Gaps

- No new fork-native path was tested or compiled for gfx1100, gfx1151 or gfx12 in this investigation.
- This research does not claim there is no possible Triton route on RDNA. The concrete issue is that certain inspected wrappers unconditionally select gfx908-specific native work for eligible shapes.

## What Caminus could borrow and the costs

### Takeaway

The strongest idea is retaining low-bit source weights while selectively caching expensive hot experts in a faster expanded compute representation, plus folding quantization into existing producer kernels. A wholesale W8A8 conversion is a poor default for a system whose served models depend on much lower-bit storage. [Expert cache](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/packed_expert_cache.py); [Local served-format capabilities](../../python/caminus/engine/k3.py)

### Cited findings

- The packed codec list does not implement Caminus's principal Q4_K/Q6_K products, nor IQ2_S/IQ3_S/IQ3_XXS/IQ4_XS. IQ2_XXS is one specific codebook format, not a synonym for IQ2_S. Caminus's format constants distinguish them and record 66 versus 82 bytes per 256 weights respectively. Thus reuse starts with matching Q8_0 or IQ2_XXS operations, or explicitly implementing additional decoders; it does not cover the catalog as-is. [Fork codec list](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py#L98-L128); [Local formats](../../python/caminus/engine/k3.py)

- Expert expansion is bounded by a configured byte budget, defaults off at zero GiB, retains source packed experts for misses and allocates INT8 up/down matrices for resident slots. Dense weights can instead be promoted to immutable INT8-only ownership, removing their packed source cache entry when the caller transfers ownership. These are distinct memory policies, not a universal full-model expansion. [Expert budget and allocation](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/packed_expert_cache.py#L299-L347); [Dense promotion](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/packed_dense_cache.py#L95-L132)

- Cached and uncached expert paths have tests demanding exact equality between their outputs/scales; they include partial tensor-parallel slices and graph replay after cache refresh. This is useful engineering evidence for cache transparency **within the fork's own GS128 math**, not equivalence to original GGUF arithmetic or Caminus model accuracy. [Cache tests](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/op_tests/test_packed_expert_cache_gfx908.py#L127-L175); [Prefill tests](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/op_tests/test_packed_prefill_gfx908.py)

### Inferences

- **Memory calculation:** GS128 with one INT8 code per weight and FP32 scale per 128 weights is 1.03125 bytes/weight. From local format sizes, this is about 1.83x Q4_K, 1.26x Q6_K, 4x IQ2_XXS and 3.22x IQ2_S, excluding metadata. A hot-expert cache adds this expanded payload while retaining compressed originals. These ratios are arithmetic from the source layouts, not measured allocations. [GS128 scale shape](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/packed_dense_cache.py#L146-L161); [Local block byte counts](../../python/caminus/engine/k3.py)
- At batch-one decode, expanding a low-bit matrix can worsen the weight-bandwidth limit even when integer arithmetic is cheaper. At higher prefill batches, shared tile decode and integer matrix throughput may amortize expansion/quantization. A cache can win if source decode costs more than the increased resident traffic; that requires matched end-to-end measurements. [Fork small-M/group loop](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/gemm_a8w8_m16_gfx908.hip#L4-L17); [Packed tile sizing](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/gemm_packed_g128.py#L153-L157)
- The easiest experimental integration is a separately launched prefill operation on an actually matching format and model shape. K3 decode would require porting the math into tape operations with its register budget, bounded waits and deterministic verify behavior, rather than calling Python dispatch inside the persistent kernel. Preserve model numerics as a separately gated mode; source-weight decode followed by GS128 requantization changes quantized math and triggers the repository's accuracy/model/self/speed requirements. [Caminus persistent architecture](../../docs/dev/architecture.md); [Own-quant gate](../../docs/specs/spec-own-quant.md); [Register/GPU rules](../../AGENTS.md)
- Producer fusion is an immediately transferable **idea**: avoid materializing floating activations solely to launch a subsequent quantizer. But choose Caminus's intended activation format and exact rounding contract; copying the fork's GS128 interface into a Q8_1 or FP16 consumer would require corresponding consumer changes. [Fork fused producer](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/gemm_silu_quant_out.py); [Local activation capabilities](../../python/caminus/engine/k3.py)
- Rank potential work as: (1) study hot-expert cache economics and producer fusion; (2) isolated matching-format prefill experiment with explicit RDNA-safe dispatch; (3) port grouped INT8 math only if profile/accuracy evidence supports it; (4) native INT8 attention as a separate quality experiment. Replacing the engine or the whole catalog is not justified by these sources. [Cache design](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/aiter/ops/triton/gemm/basic/packed_expert_cache.py); [Attention quantization stages](https://github.com/curvedinf/int8-aiter/blob/cd53c11c6d399cd79aee9500536dc5f8206f6ea3/csrc/kernels/paged_attn_decode_int8_pthead_gfx908.hip#L4-L19)

### Gaps

- No claim is made that cached INT8 improves Caminus's existing low-bit MoE latency, or that its error stays within local model gates. Neither has been measured.
- The inspected fork provides operator kernels and state/cache adapters, not a replacement implementation of Caminus's rolling conversation-window contract.
